Where to go

Seoul, Busan and Jeju: what each city is for

Most first trips run Seoul, then one more city. These are the three that repay the travel time, with a stay that matches how much there is to see.

Seoul

3–4 nights · the whole trip can happen here

  • Palaces and hanok streets: Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Ikseondong
  • Markets and shopping: Gwangjang, Namdaemun, Myeongdong, Seongsu
  • Neighbourhoods: Hongdae, Insadong, Gangnam and COEX
  • Han River parks and N Seoul Tower at dusk

Busan

2 nights · coast, food and a slower pace

  • Gamcheon Culture Village and Jagalchi fish market
  • Haeundae and Gwangalli beaches, Blueline Park sky capsule
  • Beomeosa temple in the hills, then a spa in Centum City
  • KTX from Seoul in about 2.5 hours

Jeju

3 nights · worth renting a car

  • Seongsan Ilchulbong at sunrise, Seopjikoji coast road
  • Hallasan trails and Manjanggul lava tube
  • Hyeopjae and Hamdeok beaches, O'sulloc tea fields
  • A one-hour flight from Seoul or Busan

A common nine-day route: Seoul 4 nights → Busan 2 → Jeju 3. Every extra city costs a half day in transit, so two cities are usually better than three on a week-long trip.

Practical

Five things that surprise first-time visitors

Should I bring cash or a card?

Bring a card as your main way to pay, and 50,000–100,000 won in cash as backup. Cards work almost everywhere, taxis and convenience stores included, and paying by card saves you a pocket of coins.

Cash still wins at traditional markets, small street stalls, some temple stalls and a handful of old restaurants. Buy a T-money transit card at any convenience store and load it, because not every foreign contactless card opens a subway gate.

Do I have to tip?

No. Tipping is not part of Korean culture, and the bill, the taxi meter and the café order already include service. Leaving extra money can confuse staff or look like you forgot your change, so do not add a tip on the card machine.

A few upscale hotels and international restaurants print a 10% service charge on the bill themselves; that line is already calculated for you. A tip is only usual for a tour guide or private driver on a full-day trip, and even there it is a thank-you, not an expectation.

Why does Google Maps fail here?

Korean map data is not fully exported to Google, so Google Maps has limited walking, driving and transit directions inside the country. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap instead: both have English interfaces and give accurate subway, bus and walking routes.

Paste the Korean name of a place into the search box for the best result, and keep Papago installed to translate menus and signs from a photo. Install all three before you fly.

Do I need an eSIM?

If your phone supports eSIM, it is the easiest option: buy a Korea data plan online before you fly and it starts when you land, with no queue at the airport counter. Most plans are data-only, so calls stay on your home number or move to a messenger app.

Airport counters in T1 and T2 also sell physical SIMs and pocket Wi-Fi, but they close late at night and can be busy after a long flight. Public Wi-Fi in Seoul is fast, so 5–10 GB is plenty for maps, translation and messages.

What is the duty-free allowance coming back?

Korea's allowance is US$800 of general goods per person, counted on everything you bring in, gifts included. Alcohol has its own allowance: 2 litres or less and US$400 or less in total, with no bottle limit. Tobacco is 200 cigarettes, perfume 100 ml.

Those separate allowances do not add to or eat into the US$800. Under 19, there is no alcohol or tobacco allowance, and cash over US$10,000 must be declared. If you are over the limit, declare it: doing so voluntarily cuts the duty by 30%, up to 200,000 won.

About these numbers

Every figure here is an estimate for planning, not a quote. Exchange rates are mid-market rates of 25 November 2026 and your bank will add a margin. Budget rates are typical 2026 mid-range prices in Seoul, with Busan and Jeju slightly cheaper for hotels and slightly dearer for transport. Tax refund amounts follow the published bracket table of Global Tax Free, one of Korea's refund operators; another operator, or the slip at the counter, can differ by a few thousand won.

Transit fares are Seoul Metropolitan Government fares as of 2026: subway 1,550 won by card, bus 1,500 won, transfers free within 30 minutes (60 minutes between 21:00 and 07:00), up to five rides per journey. The Climate Card 30-day pass stopped on 30 September 2026, so this page compares the 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7-day short-term passes that are on sale now.
