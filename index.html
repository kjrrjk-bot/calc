How these numbers are worked out

Estimated A1C from two readings

Your fasting and 2-hour readings are averaged 50/50 to approximate your average glucose through the day. That average is then converted with the formula used to link average glucose to A1C: A1C = (average glucose in mg/dL + 46.7) ÷ 28.7.

That formula was built for a real 3-month average from continuous monitoring, so treat our figure as a rough screen. A lab A1C is the standard and needs only one blood draw.

BMI, water and protein

BMI = 703 × weight in pounds ÷ (height in inches)².

Water = about 30 ml per kg of body weight, kept between 1.5 and 3.3 liters a day, with roughly 20% coming from food.

Protein = 1.0 to 1.2 g per kg a day, the range recommended for healthy older adults, using a weight at or below a BMI of 30 so that extra fat does not inflate the target.

Why a single high reading is not a diagnosis

One reading can be pushed up by a poor night's sleep, an infection, a steroid, a late dinner, or stress. Diagnosis needs a repeat lab test on another day. What one reading should do is get you to a doctor with a question — not to a conclusion.

Common questions

What is a normal blood sugar for a 65-year-old?

For screening, the cut-offs are the same at 65 as at 45: fasting under 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L), and under 140 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L) two hours after a meal. A fasting reading of 100–125 or a 2-hour reading of 140–199 is the prediabetes range.

If you already have diabetes, your doctor may set a higher target on purpose — very low readings are more dangerous after 65 than slightly high ones.

Can this calculator diagnose diabetes?

No. It sorts the numbers you type into the standard screening ranges and gives a rough A1C estimate. Diagnosis comes from a lab A1C, a fasting plasma glucose test, or an oral glucose tolerance test, usually repeated on a second day.

What BMI should a senior have?

Between 50 and 64, the usual healthy range is 18.5 to 24.9. From 65 on, about 23 to 27 is often considered the most comfortable range. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so also check your waist and how easily you stand up from a chair.

How much water does an older adult need?

Roughly 1.5 to 2 liters a day, about 6 to 8 cups, and part of it comes from food. Thirst weakens with age, so pale straw-colored urine is a better reminder than thirst. With heart failure, kidney disease or a diuretic, ask your doctor first.

Why is my morning reading higher than my bedtime reading?

Between about 3 and 8 a.m. your liver releases stored glucose and cortisol rises to wake you up. This is the dawn phenomenon and it is normal. A late or heavy dinner, poor sleep and some medications add to it.
